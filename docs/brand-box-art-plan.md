## Intent

Turn the approved brand street into a curated, five-panel cover-art composition. Feature ten brands chosen for recognition or a strong visual story; retain all 21 names in one expandable directory. The artwork should communicate personality while the existing headline and introduction explain Sean's thought leadership work.

Real logos stay intact. The humour comes from the scene: conspicuous consumption, everyday inconvenience and the gap between appearances and reality. No invented brand names or spoof logos. Let the jokes work visually without captions, slogans or extra marketing copy.

## Shared generation brief

Use the approved `public/images/game/brand-street/street-v1.png` as the visual reference for each generation. Match its confident ink contours, angular painted shading, warm amber light, coral sunset and lavender shadows. Continue the fictional London-meets-Vice-City setting: occasional palms, British architecture and waterfront details. Keep the same time of day and level of detail across all four scenes.

Generate each panel separately at its intended aspect ratio. Do not generate a complete collage or black borders into the assets. One dominant subject and one visual joke per scene. Reserve a clean, unobstructed, nearly frontal surface for the original brand artwork. No generated logos, text, watermarks or additional brands. Leave at least 10% breathing room around key subjects and signage so diagonal cuts remain safe. Prefer medium shots: jokes must survive the actual page size.

Add original logos in code after generation, with positions measured from the finished image. Keep the image and overlays in the same coordinate system when scaling or panning. Use mild CSS perspective only when the physical sign requires it. Do not place a corporate mark on a product that does not carry that identity; in particular, do not use the PepsiCo corporate logo as a Pepsi drinks label.

## Individual image briefs

### 1. The executive takeaway — McDonald's

**Visual:** A uniformed chauffeur stands beside the open rear door of an extravagantly long limousine, formally presenting one small paper takeaway bag on a silver tray. A passenger's jewelled hand reaches from the car. The contrast is immense ceremony for a tiny, ordinary meal.

**Framing:** 2:3 portrait, medium shot. Chauffeur and tray dominate; enough car is visible to establish the luxury without needing the whole limousine. Sunset street and a few palms are background texture. Avoid several extra characters or elaborate restaurant signage.

**Logo surface:** Broad blank front of a warm red takeaway bag, facing the camera, unobstructed by the chauffeur's fingers. Target approximately 18–22% of image width. Overlay the existing McDonald's artwork after inspecting its intrinsic colours; preserve its proportions.

**Must read at thumbnail size:** Formal service, tiny takeaway, ostentatious car. If the bag is too small to read, increase its presence before adding detail.

### 2. Behind the perfect skyline — Warner Bros.

**Visual:** A glamorous waterfront skyline turns out to be a film-set facade. At one side, the camera sees the timber supports and a stagehand holding a perfect palm tree upright. A director relaxes in a folding chair, admiring the convincing illusion.

**Framing:** 16:9 landscape, oblique view that makes both the attractive facade and its flimsy reverse visible. Keep one obvious visual reveal. No piles of cameras or extras obscuring it.

**Logo surface:** Large blank cream production-equipment case in the foreground, its broad face almost parallel to the camera. Target 18–22% of image width, with room for the existing Warner Bros. shield. Place away from the diagonal seam.

**Must read at thumbnail size:** Impressive skyline, obviously fake back, weary person supporting a palm. This is the most compositionally demanding scene, so generate it first to test the approach.

### 3. The weekend getaway — Halfords

**Visual:** An immaculate low-slung supercar parked outside an automotive shop, its roof rack overwhelmed by an absurd but carefully strapped tower of camping gear. A folded deckchair, enormous coolbox and inflatable flamingo undermine the car's glamorous image. Its owner confidently checks a packing list.

**Framing:** 16:9 landscape, three-quarter car view, with the entire overloaded silhouette visible. The shop provides context, not a second dominant subject. Use a generic unbranded car; no secondary vehicle logo needed.

**Logo surface:** Blank pale shop fascia in the upper background, occupying roughly 25–30% of image width. Keep the roof load clear of it. Add the original Halfords logo in code.

**Must read at thumbnail size:** Impractical sports car, ridiculous holiday load. Keep the load to a few oversized objects rather than dozens of tiny props.

### 4. The middle-aisle millionaire — Lidl

**Visual:** An immaculately dressed yacht guest returns along a marina pontoon with one oversized supermarket shopping bag. It overflows with an incongruous cordless drill, rolled camping mat and oversized garden gnome. A glamorous yacht and waiting companion establish the setting.

**Framing:** 2:3 portrait, full or three-quarter figure. The bag and its strange haul are the foreground focus, the yacht recedes behind. Match the reference sunset rather than switching to bright midday beach colours.

**Logo surface:** Broad flat blank face of a sturdy yellow shopping bag, turned toward the viewer. Target 24–28% of image width, without hands, folds or objects crossing the logo area. Apply the original Lidl badge afterwards.

**Must read at thumbnail size:** Yacht lifestyle meets wildly eclectic supermarket purchases. Use the garden gnome here and the flamingo on the car so the two scenes have distinct silhouettes and props.

## Brand selection

The street already covers major technology, luxury, banking, retail, utilities and media names. McDonald's and Warner Bros. add recognisable international identities; Halfords and Lidl earn their places through the visual jokes. Avoid repeating Apple or Chanel merely to fill another panel. The remaining eleven brands retain equal typographic treatment in the full directory.

Reserve ideas only if a selected scene fails: a Volkswagen camper in a comically overprepared campsite; a John Lewis delivery at a lavish penthouse with nowhere to fit the furniture. Do not generate these in the initial batch.

## Production order and review

1. Review this shortlist, humour and layout before generating more artwork.
2. Generate the Warner Bros. landscape and McDonald's portrait first, using the street as reference. These test the difficult visual reveal and the portrait treatment.
3. Fit their real logos and review them together at intended desktop and phone sizes. Revise the weakest composition before extending the batch.
4. Generate the Halfords and Lidl scenes with the accepted visual treatment. Use the garden gnome for Lidl to avoid repeating the flamingo gag.
5. Assemble all five panels with one continuous black frame and shared diagonal dividers. Keep the existing heading, copy and expandable full directory.
6. Verify logo proportions, sign alignment, safe crops, light/dark mode, keyboard access to the directory, phone panning for the street and absence of page-wide overflow. Optimise generated assets for the web while retaining originals and prompt provenance.

Success means each logo belongs to a believable illustrated surface, each new panel has a readable visual joke, and all five panels feel like one cover rather than separate advertising cards. The scene is decorative portfolio storytelling; keep existing copy factual and avoid invented testimonials, campaign claims or brand endorsements.
